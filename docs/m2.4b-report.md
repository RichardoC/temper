# temper — M2.4b report (baseline row B3: the Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct AR twin — and the instrument bug it exposed)

Milestone: **M2.4b** — the same-harness AR control row (decision doc §2 B3:
"self-normalizing: isolates diffusion-search effect from model quality").
Same 25 humaneval-rs problems, same INSTRUCTION_TEMPLATE (template sha
`bdd6144a…`, user turn byte-identical), same seeds (base_seed+i), same
T=0.2 / top_p=0.95 / K=4 / 384-token budget as the four Dream cells — but
autoregressive decoding with KV cache (`use_cache=True`) instead of a
denoising canvas. Engine: `temper/model/qwen.py` (Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-
Instruct, Apache-2.0, HF-cached, mps bf16), injected into the UNCHANGED
bench driver through its `sample_fn` point (`--engine qwen --steps 0`).
Pre-flight: two 1-sample foreground probes (probe 2: 78 tokens, 5.75 s,
13.6 tok/s, extract_ok, token-identical across repeats — commit `db64eba`).

**Headline: B3 did its job before it was even scored.** Building the
"interpretable" twin exposed a load-bearing bug in the shared
export instrument — the GOTCHA-1 stop-token stripper amputates body
fragments at column-0 closes, which converted **64–76 of every committed
cell's SyntaxError rows (all 76 in the s512 cell) from complete,
brace-balanced bodies into guaranteed parse errors**. Under the corrected
instrument the committed staircase (0.04→0.04→0.16→0.16) becomes
**0.16→0.20→0.44→0.64 honest pass@1 — 512 was never a plateau** — and the
AR twin scores **0.92**. The B3 row below is therefore reported on both
bases, and M2.3/M2.4a's budget conclusions are formally reopened (§M2.5).

## Verdict (3 lines)

1. **The matched AR twin dominates the dLLM on both axes, by a lot —
   but the honest numbers, not the committed ones.** Corrected B3 =
   **honest pass@1 = pass@4 = 0.92** (92 OK / 6 Exception / 2 SyntaxError,
   0 exclusions) in **9.35 min** of generation wall, vs corrected Dream
   s256 = 0.44 (7.21 h) and Dream s512 = 0.64 (14.48 h): B3 passes 23/25
   problems including HumanEval_17 — one of the two problems Dream could
   never even extract — and strictly extends every Dream passing set at
   every steps value measured. On the compute axis B3 used ~1.4k
   position-forwards/problem vs ~654k (s256) / ~1.31M (s512): **~470–940×
   less decode compute** for ~2.3× the corrected score.
2. **"Dream's 0.16" was neither weak nor fair — it was ~70% instrument.**
   The stop-strip amputation (§Instrument) grew with steps (64/80 → 68/92
   → 72/92 → 76/92 cut rows) precisely because more steps produce more
   complete bodies with top-level blocks for it to amputate: the M2.3
   "budget step function" and M2.4a's "cliff top, 512 strictly dominated,
   0/25 problems migrate" verdicts were measuring the artifact curve.
   Corrected, 256→512 gains **+0.20 honest pass@1** (0.44→0.64) — the
   budget response is a genuine climb with no measured plateau, and the
   canonical-B2 choice (steps=256) is reopened.
3. **With the instrument fixed, the residual engine gap is real and
   clean:** Dream's corrected failure mass (36 SyntaxError + 12 Exception
   rows at s256; 16 + 12 at s512; the 17/19 exclusions persist) is
   ordinary-model failure, not decode-order exoticism — and the AR twin's
   remaining failures are 2 problems (12 Exception×4; 19 half/half).
   Every corrected cell shows official = normalized (edition Δ 0.000) and
   repair Δ +0.000: neither the L0.5 repair pass nor the edition patch
   ever mattered on this slice, for either engine, before or after the fix.

## The instrument bug (what B3 was built to expose, and did)

**Root cause.** `export_runs` applied `strip_stop_residue` (GOTCHA 1: the
scorer does not enforce MultiPL-E stop_tokens, so a retained stop sequence
duplicates the closing brace) to the **body fragment** produced by
`to_body_fragment`. A body fragment never contains the prompted fn's own
close — but after the min-indent dedent, a TOP-LEVEL block in the fn body
(`for`/`if`/`match` at fn-body depth) legitimately lands its close at
column 0, and the `"\n}"` stop cut amputated the fragment there. An
amputated fragment inside `prompt + completion + "\n" + tests` scrambles
the block structure (the tests' leading `}` closes the wrong block) →
guaranteed `SyntaxError`. The strip was designed for whole-module
completions, where a col-0 `}` IS the fn's close; in the fragment path it
had nothing legitimate to cut and one catastrophic thing to cut.

**Measurement** (`scripts/m2_4b_strip_artifact.py`, committed artifacts
only, no GPU/container): per cell, scored rows whose exported fragment is a
strict prefix of the recomputed balanced body, cut exactly at a `\n}`
boundary — s96 **64/80**, s128 **68/92**, s256 **72/92**, s512 **76/92**
(every one of its 76 SyntaxErrors), B3 pre-fix **65/100**. "other" = 0
everywhere: every exported completion was either intact or amputated.

**Fix.** `export.py`: the multipl-e fragment path keeps only the
fence-line/blank-line trimming (a `to_body_fragment` output cannot contain
the fn close or fences by construction); the temper whole-module flavor
keeps the full stop-strip unchanged (its tests are self-contained and its
final close must survive — it was always `stop_tokens=[]` there, so no
regression surface). Regression test added: a top-level-`for` body
survives export brace-intact. Commit `5196375`.

**Audit.** All five cells re-exported + re-scored under the fixed
instrument (commit `67b1860`); `b3qwenfix-norepair` independently
re-scored a second time (`results/20261021T144648Z.json`) — statuses
identical on all 25 problems. Corrected exclusions match the committed
ones exactly (s256/s512: 17×4 + 19×4 — those two are REAL model failures,
unchanged; s96: 16 on the phase0fix basis). All committed M2.2/M2.3/M2.4a
artifacts remain untouched; the corrected rows live in new
`exports/20261021T14*fix-*` dirs + `results/` files.

## Is Dream's 0.16 weak or fair for a matched 7B?

Three-part answer:

1. **As committed, 0.16 was not a measurement of Dream at all.** 72 of the
   92 scored s256 rows were amputated complete bodies; the model's own
   failure mass at s256 was 36 SyntaxError + 12 Exception + 8 excluded
   (corrected basis). The committed 0.16 (and the 0.83 "compile-dead"
   share, and the "budget step function", and "512 = plateau") is
   instrument history. Anything built on those numbers — including M2.3's
   two rejected candidates and M2.4a's steering target list — must be
   re-derived (§M2.5).
2. **Corrected, Dream's 0.44 (s256) / 0.64 (s512) is fair as an unsteered
   dLLM row** — it is what Dream-Coder-7B actually does under its
   publisher-recommended config on this harness, and the corrected
   per-problem failure sets are stable and explicable (see below).
3. **But it is weak for the matched 7B pair.** Same instruction text, same
   seeds, same T/top_p, same token budget, same container: the AR twin
   scores 0.92 honest pass@1 — +0.28 over Dream's best measured point
   (s512, at 9.3× B3's wall) and +0.48 over the canonical B2 point (at
   46× B3's wall, ~470× the position-forwards). The gap is
   **model×decoding-paradigm, not model quality, not prompt, not harness**:
   the twin solves 8 problems Dream-s512 never solves in any seed, shares
   all of Dream-s512's other 15 passers, and the only problem Dream
   "wins" is none — s512's passer set is a strict subset of B3's.
   Unsteered diffusion decoding carries a large, real tax on Rust code
   generation at this scale; diffusion SEARCH (L1's premise) now has to
   beat an AR control that is already at 0.92 (§M2.5).

## NFE accounting and the honest natural-comparisons caveat

**The NFE units are NOT commensurable, and both axes are reported.**

- *dLLM NFE* (one per denoise step): a FULL-canvas forward — prompt
  (~254 tok mean, Dream tokenizer) + 384 canvas positions re-encoded every
  step. s256 = 1024 NFE/problem ≈ **653,681 position-forwards/problem**;
  s512 ≈ 1,307,361.
- *AR NFE* (B3): `nfe` = generated tokens (83.2 mean/sample; 8323/problem
  total), `nfe_prefill` = 100 (one per sample), `n_forward_total` = 8323
  (= nfe + nfe_prefill − 1 per sample: the prefill forward produces the
  first token — the record carries all three fields so no derivation is
  needed). In position-forwards: 4 prefills × ~264 tok + 8223 × 1 ≈
  **1,386/problem** — the decode forwards are [1,1] with KV cache.
- Ratio: **~470× (s256) / ~943× (s512)** — and this understates the dLLM
  side, whose per-step attention re-processes the whole canvas.
- **Wall-clock (the second axis):** B3 generation 561.07 s total =
  22.4 s/problem = 5.6 s/sample (LS fit over the record: prefill 269 ms,
  decode 64.2 ms/token ≈ 15.6 tok/s, mean 83.2 tokens/sample, all
  EOS-terminated ≤176 tokens of the 384 budget) vs Dream s256 7.21 h and
  s512 14.48 h → **46× / 93×**. Scoring adds 10–23 s/container-run for
  either engine. Any cross-engine claim in this project must carry both
  columns; per the decision doc's protocol, NFE stays the nominal x-axis
  with the unit mismatch stated, wall-clock the honest tiebreaker.
- Scoring container: Rancher Desktop (Server 29.5.3, amd64 rustc 1.75.0
  under Rosetta), started ~20 s after launch, only after ps-verified model
  exit and 94% memory free; quit + verified down (0 processes, socket
  gone) after each scoring phase. Generation ran with Rancher closed
  throughout (guardrail 1); all waits were in-flight sleep-polls.

## B3 generation health and per-problem structure

- 100/100 rows, **0 gen errors, 0 truncations, extract_ok 100/100**
  pre-score (dLLM cells: 80–92/100). Every sample EOS-terminated (mean 83.2
  tokens, min 22, max 176 of 384) — the 384 token budget was never the
  binding constraint, unlike the dLLM canvas where truncation/exclusion
  classes (17 prose-echo, 19 mask-corruption) were structural.
- Corrected B3 failures: **HumanEval_12** (compiles, wrong output ×4) and
  **HumanEval_19** (2 wrong-output + 2 SyntaxError — the `number_map`
  attempt). 23/25 problems pass 4/4.
- Engine dominance chain on this slice: B3 ⊇ corrected-s512 ⊇
  corrected-s256 ⊇ corrected-s128 ⊇ corrected-s96 passer sets, with B3
  additionally solving HumanEval_17 (Dream's deterministic prose-echo
  exclusion) outright. No problem is solved by Dream and missed by B3.
- Seed diversity at T=0.2 (raw-sha method, m2.4a-compatible): 9/25 B3
  problems byte-identical across K=4 (median 2 distinct, one problem
  4-distinct) vs 13/25 at Dream s256 — comparable collapse; with p@1=p@4
  in every corrected cell, best-of-K selection has nothing to select at
  K=4 for either engine. Exploration, not wider K, remains the lever.

## M2.5 notes (what the twin says L1 steering needs to demonstrate)

1. **Re-derive the substrate under the corrected instrument first.** The
   corrected staircase (0.16 / 0.20 / 0.44 / 0.64, still rising at 512)
   invalidates the M2.4a steering target list as written: at s256 the
   "83% compile-dead mass" is 39%, the 17/19 exclusions persist but 17 is
   solved by the AR twin outright, and the "diversity collapse kills BoN"
   claim now applies to both engines (p@1=p@4 everywhere at K=4).
2. **The bar for L1-on-Dream is now quantified and steep:** steering must
   beat the unsteered corrected curve (0.44 @ 1024 NFE/problem, 7.2 h) by
   enough to justify its own NFE — against a same-harness AR control at
   0.92 in 9.4 min. "L1 makes the dLLM competitive with its AR twin" is
   the strongest available framing; "L1 beats unsteered Dream" alone is
   no longer interesting.
3. **Consider B3 (or AR generally) as the L1 substrate.** The twin's
   remaining failures (2/25) leave little headroom on this slice, but the
   full 156-problem humaneval-rs (and mbpp-rs) leave plenty; AR rows are
   ~470× cheaper per problem, so steering sweeps that were wall-prohibited
   on the dLLM are cheap on the twin. Verifier-signal resampling (the
   FK/SMC design) applies unchanged — extract/compile signals still fire
   (B3 has 2 SyntaxError + 6 Exception rows to kill).
4. **Protocol carry-forwards, now load-bearing:** (a) every cross-engine
   table must show wall next to NFE (§NFE); (b) the export instrument is
   now regression-tested against top-level-block amputation — keep it
   that way; (c) before any M2.5 budget claim, decide whether to re-baseline
   the canonical B2 point at s512 (corrected +0.20 hon p@1 for 2× wall —
   the M2.4a "strictly dominated" verdict is inverted).
5. **The natural-comparison caveat stays attached to every published
   number:** AR NFE unit ≠ dLLM NFE unit; the defensible cross-engine
   axes are position-forwards, wall-clock, and cost — report all three.
