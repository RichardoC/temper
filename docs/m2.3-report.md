## Verdict (3 lines)

1. **The honest 0.04 WAS a decode-budget artifact — and the budget response
   is THRESHOLD-shaped, not linear**: honest pass@1 = 0.04 → 0.04 → **0.16**
   across 96 → 128 → 256 steps (official 0.050 → 0.043 → 0.174; honest
   pass@4 identical to pass@1 in every cell). 96→128 moved NOTHING (the
   artifact persisted); 128→256 moved 4×. "Capability floor" and
   "budget-linear" are both rejected by the data.
2. **The corruption disease partially CLEARS at 256**: SyntaxError share of
   scored 0.95 → 0.96 → **0.83** (76/92 scored rows still fail to compile),
   and the pass set broadened from strlen-only to **4 problems × 4/4 OK**
   (HumanEval_2_truncate_number, 7_filter_by_substring, 15_string_sequence,
   23_strlen) — including HumanEval_15, the phase-0 doc-echo disease problem,
   which went corrupt@96 → extracted-but-SyntaxError@128 → **passing@256**.
   The s128 disease problems (24/25) also RECOVERED at 256; the 8 exclusions
   moved to HumanEval_17/19 (prose-echo / adjacent-token corruption
   `Vec<&'>` — the repetition-corruption class). M2.2's forensic prediction
   ("SyntaxError share collapses at 256") was directionally right and
   quantitatively partial: the collapse is 0.96 → 0.83 with a 4× pass@1,
   not to zero.
3. **A second, budget-insensitive disease remains**: 8/100 excluded rows in
   BOTH the 128 and 256 cells (different problems — a per-cell lottery), 0.83
   of scored rows still SyntaxError at 256, repair delta +0.000 in all three
   cells, edition delta 0.000 in all three cells. Decode budget at these
   levels fixes the adjacent-token-commitment corruption class but not the
   repetition/shape class — that residue is what steering (L1) and template
   hardening must attack, not more steps.

## Answer to "was M2.2's 0.04 a decode-budget artifact?"

**YES — but not linearly: the budget response is threshold-shaped.** The full
ablation: honest pass@1 identical (0.04) at 96 AND 128 steps with the SAME
pass set (strlen only), then **0.16 at 256** with a 4-problem pass set and
the SyntaxError share dropping 0.96 → 0.83. Of the three candidate answers:
- *budget-linear* — rejected: 96→128 doubled NFE with zero effect.
- *budget-plateaued-at-128* — rejected: the plateau BROKE at 256 (4×).
- *capability floor* — rejected: 0.04 → 0.16 with everything held constant
  except steps (same template sha, seeds, T, K, canvas, extractor, scorer).

The mechanism is the one M2.2's forensics named: at coarse commit schedules
(few tokens committed per denoise step × high per-step token yield), the
masked-diffusion decoder commits adjacent-token corruption mid-expression
(`numbers[i + 1]` → `numbers + 1]`, `Vec<&'>` for `Vec<&str>`); at 256
tokens-per-step yield drops enough that the coherent-completion fraction
quadruples. The 0.83 residual SyntaxError share + the budget-insensitive
repetition/shape exclusions are a SECOND disease (decode-order incoherence
and prompt-shape failures) that more steps does not fix.

Caveat worn openly: single seed group (seeds 0–3), 25 problems, K=4, T=0.2.
The threshold location (between 128 and 256) is bracketed, not pinned;
a steps=192 cell or a seed-replicated 256 cell would sharpen it (M2.4).

## M2.4 notes (inputs for the next milestone)

1. **Qwen2.5-Coder-7B twin (B3) — sized and unblocked**: run B3 at
   **steps=256** (the winning config; decision doc: both twins get the same
   steps config). Measured cost of a 256 cell is **7.21h** (not the 16.5h
   projected from naive 2× scaling — the fixed-shape sampler removed the
   driver-bloat drag; per-step ~1.04 s at canvas 384). Start the ~15 GB
   Qwen download in the background of the next run.
2. **L1 steering design — the ablation says SMC/FK plausibly beats the
   disease class**: the ≤128-steps disease is per-canvas decode-dynamics
   corruption, which is (a) OBSERVABLE (extract failure / SyntaxError /
   must_define rejection are cheap fitness signals) and (b) heterogeneous
   across canvases (K=4 seeds produce 2–4 distinct texts) — exactly the
   setting where sequential-MC resampling on a verifier signal beats
   unsteered BoN: corrupt particles get killed instead of consuming the
   whole budget. Two design consequences: (i) fitness must include the
   cheap structural signals (extractability, must_define, compile) BEFORE
   test execution — at 256, 83% of canvases are structurally dead weight;
   (ii) the MPS compile-cache fix is load-bearing for L1: steering runs
   many more canvases per problem, and the native sampler's linear driver
   growth would have hit the 335 GB cap mid-run at high NFE. Keep
   `dream_fixed` active (kill-switch: TEMPER_NATIVE_DREAM_SAMPLE=1).
3. **Cheap next ablations**: (a) steps=192 or 384 to bracket the threshold
   (the jump lives between 128 and 256); (b) seed replication (base_seed
   100) for error bars before hard capability claims — one seed group is a
   small sample and the exclusion lottery moves per cell.
4. **Repair stays 0-delta** (+0.000 in all three cells at aggregate level):
   the corruption classes (span drops, renames, repetition) are not
   conservatively repairable. Keep the ablation column; expect salvage to
   matter only on steered/templated rows (M1.5 pattern).
5. **Template A/B remains a labeled-cell option**: prose-echo exclusions
   (17@256, 15@96) would likely fall to a response-format contract
   ("output MUST begin with the fn line"); scored fraction would rise
   92/100 → ~100/100. Its own cell, not a silent change.
6. **Operational guardrails that worked**: generation with containers
   closed; scoring only after ps-verified model exit + memory check; commit
   at every phase boundary (the mid-run fix commit `aaf939a` is what made
   the 3×-relaunched s256 cell recoverable within budget).
