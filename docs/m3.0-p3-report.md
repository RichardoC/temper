### Seed scheme — clarification, not deviation

The standing guardrail is "seed_paired_across_temps semantics where ladders
serve multiple temps". At P3's ladder that semantics degenerates, and the
arithmetic forks three ways:

| option | seeds | problem |
|---|---|---|
| **A: FLAT** | `base_seed+i` = [0…7] | **chosen** |
| B: full pairing (`--seed-paired-across-temps`, K=1) | all 8 canvases get **seed 0** | P2 proved the seed path is the only RNG source ⇒ the two T=0.80 canvases are **byte-identical**, likewise the two T=0.95 ones. 2 of 8 canvases per problem are provable duplicates = 25 % of a 14.9 h cell redrawn |
| C: grouped pairing | per-temp seed {0,1} | 8 distinct canvases, but the comparability it preserves is consumed by nobody here (P1's pairing exists to isolate temperature at K=4 *per temp* with a reachability gate; P3's slots are independent configurations with 1–2 canvases per temp) |

Supervisor ruling: **(A) FLAT**. The rev2 doc named *temperatures*, not seeds,
so this is a clarification. FLAT also keeps game 0 bit-anchored to the banked
s256 anchor, making the ship gate's zero-regression check on the 11-passer
cohort **mechanical rather than statistical**.

**Verified on the real canvases, all 25 problems:** every T=0.2/seed-0 sidecar
is byte-identical (sha256) to the corresponding `20261020T100251Z-s256.jsonl.raw`
p0 sidecar — **25/25 identical**.

### Three findings that shape the rest of P3

**1. C-null (14) is BELOW anchor-2048 (16), and the lost problems are exactly
the budget-fixable cohort.** 10, 12 and 25 were all won by the unsteered s512
anchor and all three are lost by the ladder. This is the design's own caveat
materialising: s512 spends 2048 NFE on **four 512-step** canvases (fine commit
grid); the ladder spends the same 2048 on **eight 256-step** canvases (coarser
grid, more draws). The ladder buys *diversity* and pays in *commit
granularity*, and on the budget-fixable cohort granularity was what those
problems needed. The control is therefore **not a free win over the anchors** —
which is precisely what review B's 13–16/25 null bet predicted (measured: 14,
dead centre).

**2. The ladder's single gain over anchor-2048 is HumanEval_17 (+1)**, the
exclusion-pair problem the P1 probe found reachable at T=0.8. Per the
`m3.0-report.md` warning, C-null reproduces it, so **a 17 flip can never count
as C-full mechanism evidence**. The attribution gate's outside-cohort
requirement is live and stricter than the design text assumed.

**3. Selection, not reachability, is the binding constraint.** Among the eight
canvases per problem, **16/25 problems have at least one container-OK canvas**
(the oracle), but **both rules pick only 14**. And they miss *different* ones:

| rule | available but missed |
|---|---|
| C-null | HumanEval_24 (4 OK canvases, emitted a T=0.2 Exception), HumanEval_25 (1 OK, emitted a T=0.95 Exception) |
| C-screen | HumanEval_1 (1 OK at T=0.2, emitted a T=0.8 Exception), HumanEval_9 (2 OK, emitted an export-excluded T=0.95) |

The screen is **not** an oracle and slightly mis-ranks: confidence ranks a
compile-clean-but-semantically-wrong canvas above a compile-clean-and-correct
one. **Two machines already see 16/25; the emission rule forfeits 2.** This is
the single most actionable measurement in P3 for M3.1, and it also means the
primary C-full − C-screen delta is partly a *rule* effect unless C-full's
ensemble coincides with C-null's — recorded as a forensics precondition.

### The crash, and what it taught

Generation died after 19 games at **HumanEval_19_sort_numbers** (the 20th list
entry — not "problem 20"). The D2 meter ruled out mask-budget exhaustion: all
384 positions committed, mask_share 0.0 at every checkpoint.

Root cause: Dream's tokenizer maps `[0, 151666]` and returns `None` for every
id in `[151667, 152064)` — **397 contiguous slots**, 0.3 % of `vocab_size`. They
are not special tokens, so `skip_special_tokens` cannot drop them, and **both**
decode sites raise `TypeError`. The suspect-fix ("guard `_render_canvas`") was
escalated rather than shipped, because the emission site (`_finish`) is exposed
identically — it builds the text the container scores — so a render-only guard
would have crashed one call later, after the game had spent its full 2048 NFE.
The supervisor confirmed the wider scope.

Fix: one shared `_decodable_ids` helper at **both** sites, dropping unmappable
ids before `tokenizer.decode`; vendored tokenizer untouched; EOS-cut kept
upstream; special-token policies unchanged. Dropping is honest — an id with no
text form cannot exist in any container-scored source, and aborting would turn
a tokenizer artifact into a scored failure the unsteered baseline never faced.
The artifact is now **measured**: D2 logs dropped ids separately per site.

The guard fired on the real path: the whole 25-game cell contains exactly
**one** unmappable id (151692, one particle of HumanEval_19), dropped at both
sites. **C-null exposure verified 0/200** (200/200 decodes completed, every
sidecar round-trips inside the mappable range), so the pair is provably
symmetric on this artifact.

Disposition: rows 0–18 predate the fix and were **not** re-rendered (their own
`_finish` succeeded). The six missing problems {19,20,21,23,24,25} were
regenerated into the same out-path. Game 19 was **not** recoverable offline —
the D2 log stores `canvas_prefix_sha256`, not id arrays — so recovery was
correctly abandoned in favour of regeneration.

### An earlier R0 defect, also found here

`_render_canvas` decoded with `skip_special_tokens=False` (required — mask
residue must reach `fill_holes`) but did not cut at EOS. Finished canvases
therefore rendered `<|endoftext|>` as text after the closing fence, so
`prose_after_fence` fired on **92/104** final checkpoints; the same canvases on
their emitted text flagged **2/13** (both genuine: HumanEval_1, 9). A ~45 %
false-kill rate on R0, **outcome-neutral** under the demoted config (no kills
executed) but fatal to any re-enabled kill rung. Fixed and pinned.

## VERDICT against the gates

| gate | criterion | result |
|---|---|---|
| **SHIP** | ≥15/25 + zero net regressions on the 11 + ≥2 confirmed-attributed flips | **PASS** — 17/25, 0 regressions, 2 confirmed-attributed (1, 9) |
| **ATTRIBUTION** | > max(nulls) by ≥+2, ≥1 outside-cohort flip, replication green | **FAIL** — margins +3/+3 ✓, outside flip present (25) ✓, **replication not green** |
| **STRONG** | ≥18/25 | **FAIL** — 17/25 |

**The capability line clears its gate; the causal story does not fully close.**

What is established: the engine beats both nulls by +3 on the pre-registered
primary, with zero regressions; C-full is the best cell the program has
produced (17 vs 16); the delta is not an emission-rule artifact; two flips
replicate outside the ladder-reachable cohorts.

What is NOT established: the single outside-cohort flip the attribution
argument most needed — HumanEval_25, budget-fixable and P1-**unreachable**, the
one problem neither knowledge delivery nor the ladder alone ever produced —
**failed to reproduce**. The two attributed flips that did replicate (1, 9) are
both problems where C-null's emitted canvas was an export-exclusion or a
mis-ranked pick, i.e. **selection-quality** wins rather than mid-decode
mechanism.

That asymmetry is reported rather than smoothed. Design §4.2's own guard is
exactly what fired: the replication clause is what stops "+3" from being
oversold as mechanism.

## C-full − C-screen vs review B's bet

Review B predicted **C-full − C-screen ∈ {0, +1}**. Measured: **+3**. The bet
is refuted in the engine's favour — but the honest caveat is that 2 of the 3
come from selection-quality on problems where the offline screen mis-picked,
and only HumanEval_4 is a type-quartet flip. So review B's *mechanism*
pessimism survives at the level of replicated attribution even though its
*number* was too low.

## Recommendation for M3.1

1. **Do not claim the mechanism yet.** Ship the engine, ship 17/25, and state
   the attribution gate as FAILED with its exact clause. The program's own
   pre-registration says to report it that way.
2. **Selection is the live lever, not kill/clone.** PHASE B measured an oracle
   of 16/25 for C-null against 14/25 picked — two machines already see what the
   emission rule forfeits. C-full's +3 comes from better draws plus better
   picks. M3.1's property-grade ranking attacks exactly this, and it is where
   the measured headroom is.
3. **Re-run the attribution question with more draws per problem, not more
   problems.** The binding limit is that one Bernoulli per problem cannot
   confirm a flip; the replication design (2 games) already showed 4/5. A
   flip-set replication at 4 games/problem would decide 25 either way.
4. **C-kill stays dropped** (§4.2 cut order) and the kill-clone re-enable must
   clear the R0 fix first — the 45 % false-kill rate measured here is the
   reason that rung is not safe to turn on.
